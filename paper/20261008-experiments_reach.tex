\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We train $\pi_U$ and $\pi_L$ in Isaac Lab~\cite{mittal2025isaaclab} with the MAPPO implementation of
SKRL~\cite{serrano2023skrl}, with 4096 environments and a 50\,Hz policy over a 200\,Hz physics step, for
\wx{N environment steps} per run, one seed per $\lambda \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$. All other settings are shared across
runs. The evaluation below holds every network and normalizer fixed and freezes the depth curriculum.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\textbf{Goals.} Every controller is scored on one fixed set of \reach{goals}{n} two-wrist goals. We draw
\reach{goals}{bases} pairs of wrist poses from the standing-reachable tables of Sec.~\ref{sec:upper} and lower each
pair by $d \in \{0, 0.1, \dots, \reach{goals}{depthmax}\}$\,m, the $z_{\mathrm{offset}}$ of training. To test reach away from
the body as well as below it, each lowered pair is also pushed $e \in \{0, 0.1, 0.2\}$\,m outward in the ground
plane, away from each shoulder. The lowest pose in the standing tables is \reach{goals}{standingfloor}\,cm above the
ground; a goal whose lower wrist target lies below that height cannot be reached without the legs. Goals are placed
in the standing frame at the moment they start and stay fixed in the world for 4\,s, as in training.

\textbf{Trials.} A trial resets the robot with the training reset distribution, holds a zero velocity command for
2\,s, then presents one goal. Policies act through their mean actions with observation noise on, and the learned
pelvis estimator moves the goal they observe (Sec.~\ref{sec:estimator}). Each controller runs
\reach{l1/isaac}{n} trials in Isaac Lab, \reach{l1/isaac}{pergoal} per goal, with the training physics at its
nominal values and pushes off.

\textbf{Metrics.} A trial succeeds when both wrists stay within 5\,cm and 0.35\,rad of their true world-frame goals
for 1\,s without a break, the criterion of the reach bonus; success within 10\,cm applies the same test at 10\,cm
and 0.6\,rad. We report success with 95\% Wilson intervals, the wrist
error over the last second of successful trials, and the pelvis height over that second. For balance we report the
signed distance of the centre of mass and of the divergent component of motion
$\xi = c_{xy} + \dot c_{xy}/\omega$, $\omega = \sqrt{g/z_c}$, to the edge of the support polygon over the goal's
last second, the foot lift-offs (steps) during the goal, the base tilt, and falls (tilt beyond 1\,rad). The
operational floor of a controller is the lowest 0.1\,m band of lower target height from which every band above it
has at least 80\% success on the goals without extension; its 10\,cm floor is the lowest band in which at least half
of those goals succeed within 10\,cm.

\textbf{Controllers.} We compare the three $\lambda$ runs with three references. \emph{Blind legs} runs the
$\lambda = 1$ networks but gives $\pi_L$ the observation it has while standing in navigation mode, without the
wrist goals; its lower body balances and does not cooperate. \emph{IK arms} holds $\pi_U$'s residual at zero, so the
arms follow the damped-least-squares step alone. \emph{Whole-body agent} is one MAPPO agent that drives all 26 joints
through the same two action terms, observes both agents' observations and is paid every term of both agents' own
rewards once, with the same commands, curriculum, estimator and training budget. \emph{GOLEM IK} is the upper-body
controller of our lab's deployed
stack, a quadratic-programming IK over the 14 arm joints with self-collision avoidance, solved on a standing robot
whose pelvis cannot move.

\subsection{Effect of reward sharing}
\label{sec:sharing}

Over all goals, $\lambda = 1$, $0.5$ and $0$ reached \reach{l1/isaac}{successten}\%,
\reach{l0.5/isaac}{successten}\% and \reach{l0/isaac}{successten}\% within 10\,cm
(\reach{l1/isaac}{success}\%, \reach{l0.5/isaac}{success}\% and \reach{l0/isaac}{success}\% under the strict test),
with 10\,cm floors of \reach{l1/isaac}{floorten}, \reach{l0.5/isaac}{floorten} and \reach{l0/isaac}{floorten}\,cm.
All three runs crouch during arm goals: for goals below the tables their median pelvis drops were
\reach{l1/isaac}{drop_below}, \reach{l0.5/isaac}{drop_below} and \reach{l0/isaac}{drop_below}\,cm. Sharing changes
how the crouch follows the goal. For goals above the tables the $\lambda = 0$ pelvis stayed
\reach{l0/isaac}{drop_above}\,cm low, against \reach{l0.5/isaac}{drop_above} and \reach{l1/isaac}{drop_above}\,cm at
$\lambda = 0.5$ and $1$, whose pelvis height rises with the target (Fig.~\ref{fig:reach}c). Within 10\,cm the
$\lambda = 0$ policy reached the most goals below the tables (\reach{l0/isaac}{successten_below}\%, against
\reach{l0.5/isaac}{successten_below}\% and \reach{l1/isaac}{successten_below}\%) and the fewest above
(\reach{l0/isaac}{successten_above}\%, against \reach{l0.5/isaac}{successten_above}\% and
\reach{l1/isaac}{successten_above}\%); above the tables $\lambda = 1$ matched GOLEM IK
(\reach{golem_ik/kinematic}{successten_above}\%). Full sharing cost the legs their own task: on eight walking
commands (0.2--0.5\,m/s forward, backward and sideways, and turns), the velocity error was
\reach{l1/walk}{err_xy}\,cm/s at $\lambda = 1$, whose legs stood still, against \reach{l0.5/walk}{err_xy} and
\reach{l0/walk}{err_xy}\,cm/s at $\lambda = 0.5$ and $0$.

\subsection{Sim-to-sim transfer}
\label{sec:sim2sim}

We run the same goals in MuJoCo on the model of our lab's RoboCasa simulation, through a reimplementation of the
policy loop that reproduces the training environment's observations to $10^{-5}$ and its joint targets to
$2\times10^{-5}$\,rad from the same state, without retuning. With
RoboCasa's joint damping, armature and friction the $\lambda = 1$ policy reached \reach{l1/mujoco}{success}\%
of all goals and \reach{l1/mujoco}{success_below}\% of those below the tables, with
\reach{l1/mujoco}{falls}\% falls; with those three joint parameters set to the training values it reached
\reach{l1/mujoco_isaacphys}{success}\%. % RESULT: name the gap and its main cause.
